§01 · MASTER MARK Concept 01 · Document Stamp · Production refinement
paper · full specification
dark · restricted state
Why this mark won

The square hallmark format is the oldest Western authentication signal. Not a brand shape. Not a document icon. A certification instrument. The double frame creates a containment hierarchy: the outer border is the formal container, the inner border is the authenticated zone, the λ is the certified content. Corner registration marks are process residue — they signal the mark was aligned and verified before application.

Anatomy

Outer frame: ink (#171612), 1.2px stroke. Inner frame: brass (#7A5B1B), 0.6px stroke — half weight of outer, creates depth without competing. Lambda: Georgia italic, brass, centered. Corner squares: 3×3px solid ink, positioned at exact corners — they frame the mark without touching it. Total mark area: 80×80px canonical, scales from 16px to any print dimension.

The corner squares — pressure test result

Tested with and without. Without corners: the mark reads as a branded monogram. With corners: the mark reads as a hallmark. The corners win because the brief requires institutional authentication, not monogram branding. At sizes below 24px the corners collapse to a single pixel or disappear — this is acceptable and specified in the reduction rules.

§02 · SECONDARY MARKS Official seal · Proof mark · Usage hierarchy
CONCEPT 06 The Notary Seal
paper
dark
Role

Document watermark on cover pages. Certificate stamps. NDA cover sheet. Diligence-room access artifact. Appendix certification. The text-on-path ring carries the document reference — self-referential provenance. Not used as everyday identity.

Rule

Never used as primary brand mark. Never used at sizes below 80px — the text-on-path is illegible below that point and the mark loses its distinction from a simple circular logo. At 7% opacity on cover backgrounds; at 40–60% for certification stamps.

Role

Proof-passed state indicator. Validation badge on reports and exhibits. Verification UI in product interfaces. Favicon alternative. Quality seal on appendix covers. ■ QED — proof complete, no further argument required.

Rule

Never competes with the master mark in the same layout zone. Used as a system icon or state indicator, not as brand identity. The outer ring is optional at small sizes — the inner filled square plus void reads alone at 12px and above.

§03 · HIERARCHY When to use which mark
PRIMARY MARK
Document Stamp — used everywhere
Website nav, email signatures, business cards, invoices, procurement packets, partner decks, product shell headers, legal documents, presentation covers, merchandise. The mark that bears the company across all surfaces and all futures.
OFFICIAL SEAL
Notary Seal — ceremonial and issuing contexts only
Document watermarks, NDA cover sheets, diligence-room artifacts, certificate stamps, appendix certifications, controlled-review headers. Never used as everyday identity. Never under 80px.
PROOF MARK
QED Block — proof states and validation contexts
Validated exhibits, proof-passed indicators, quality seals in reports, verification UI in product interfaces, favicon alternative. A state signal, not an identity element. Reads at 12px.
