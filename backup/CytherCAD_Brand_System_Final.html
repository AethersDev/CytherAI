Section 1 — Construction Geometry
Mark Derivation

Every element traces to a system property. The mark is derived, not designed. Three construction principles govern every proportion. No element exists for aesthetic reasons. The geometry is a proof of the system's architecture rendered visually.

Principle 1 — Constraint Envelope
Hexagonal boundary. Six vertices.
Width = height / φ.

Closed form — the FSM state space is finite and enumerable. The six sides are the six faces of the constraint closure. No opening, no ambiguity.
Principle 2 — Tri-Layer Convergence
Three verification layers converge at one point: the artifact.

L1 = FSM · L2 = DOF · L3 = OCCT. The output exists only where all three layers agree. The convergence point is the gold dot — the only geometrically necessary point in the mark.
Principle 3 — Convergence as Proof
The checkmark is eliminated. The convergence point is the proof. Three constraint layers meeting at one point is a stronger formal claim than a consumer verification icon. The gold dot does not say "approved" — it says "this is the only point where the constraints agree."
Boundary shapeRegular hexagon — six vertices, six sides. Width = height / φ. Proportions are invariant.
Beam origin positionsTwo upper vertices (top-left, top-right) and one lower vertex (bottom center). Asymmetric by position — the bottom beam is vertical; the upper beams are diagonal.
Convergence pointAbove geometric center — positioned at optical center (slightly above mathematical center). This echoes the CytherAI crown's optical centering principle.
Stroke linecapNo linecap specified for beams (default butt) — they terminate at the convergence point and origin nodes. The miter join on the boundary is structural.
No checkmarkEliminated from all registers. The convergence point is the verification statement. A checkmark introduces consumer iconography into an engineering mark.
φ governanceWidth/height ratio and convergence point positioning governed by φ = 1.618. The geometry should feel inevitable, not designed.

Section 5 — Semantic Encoding
What the Mark Says
◈ Encoded meaning

The hexagonal boundary is the FSM state space — closed, finite, six-vertex.
The three convergence beams are the three verification layers — FSM, DOF, OCCT.
The central convergence point is the only place all three agree — the verified artifact.

The mark does not depict the product. It encodes the product's proof structure.
Strip the wordmark: an engineer reads "constrained convergence."
Strip the geometry: an observer reads "formal authority, not startup energy."

∎ derived from architecture · not designed from aesthetic preference
Why hexagon · not circle or square

A circle implies infinite possibility — the opposite of a bounded constraint system. A square implies orthogonal simplicity. A hexagon has six faces — one for each vertex of closure — and is the most efficient tiling of planar space. It reads as engineered, not organic. The form earns its geometry.

Why three beams · not two or four

Two beams would suggest a binary choice. Four would dilute the specificity. Three is the exact number of constraint layers in the CytherCAD architecture: FSM, DOF, OCCT. The geometry is a proof of the stack. Any change to the architecture requires a re-derivation of the mark.

Why the convergence point · not a checkmark

A checkmark is a consumer symbol — it says "done." The convergence point says "all three constraints agree at this location." That is a stronger and more specific claim. The dot is not approval. It is the artifact itself — the single point of constraint satisfaction.

Register transition rule

A document may open in the Sovereign Register (this mark, the wordmark) and transition to the Operational Register for its body. The mark introduces. The instrument system executes. They are not two styles — they are one theorem expressed in two notations.

CytherCAD is an engineering artifact compiler with proof-carrying output — and the proof is enforced during generation by an architecture whose correctness is demonstrated by the structure of its failures.

Section 6 — Mark Governance
Legal and Illegal States

The mark is a constrained system. Its rules derive from the same theorem that governs the product: illegal states must be structurally unrepresentable, not merely discouraged.

Permitted backgrounds

Void black (#000000) for sovereign contexts. Parchment (#F3F1EC) for operational contexts. Dark warm (#0E0C09) for cover pages. No colored surfaces.

Permitted sizes

Mark at any scale ≥ 24px boundary height. Below 24px use only the boundary outline — no internal elements. Never scale the mark below legibility.

Permitted contexts

Cover pages, patent filings, formal headings, investor covers, paper headers. Transition: sovereign cover → operational body is the correct document hierarchy.

Illegal — Mark states

Mark on colored backgrounds. Mark inside operational interfaces (dashboards, nav bars). Boundary separated from internal geometry. Convergence beams without boundary. Any element used independently.

Illegal — Checkmark

The verification checkmark is eliminated from all registers. It was present in early versions as a whispered claim. It is now absent. The convergence point is the proof. Do not reintroduce the checkmark in any form, at any opacity, in any context.

Illegal — Typography

Cormorant Garamond in operational artifacts. Barlow Condensed or sans-serif in sovereign contexts. Wordmark without mark geometry. Tagline without wordmark. Any gradient on the wordmark.
