Make it safe to let AI change real systems.

When a number in your data looks wrong, ADII investigates and answers: fix it, leave it, or escalate it. The AI that proposes a fix can’t approve it. Two programs outside the model decide whether the change is allowed and whether it holds.

A research project from CytherAI. No evaluation results are published and no deployment is claimed. What follows is a recorded run.

The answer

Yes. Fix it. A fix was proposed, it is within what ADII may change here, and an independent rebuild accepted it. The AI that proposed it didn’t approve it.

In the investigator’s words: The DATA-97 staging filter excludes all Juniper and Kiln orders from 2026-08-18 onward. On August 18, those distributors had 55 acknowledged raw orders worth $2,799.94, exactly the gap between raw (111 orders, $5,541.40) and staged/mart (56 orders, $2,741.46). The vendor receipt and successful load both report 111 rows; prior days also include these distributors. The web release is not the cause.

The details are then opened and scrolled through.

Daily revenue

$2,741, ↓51% against the 14 days before: daily revenue as the system read it before anything was investigated. A chart from Aug 4 to Aug 18: nearly flat, then a sharp drop on Aug 18. “What looked wrong: the alerted number, read from the data when the run began. It is the symptom, not evidence.”

Independent rebuild

The validator is a separate program. It rebuilt the data from a frozen copy with the change applied and read the number again. The investigator never sees this. A chart compares the number before with the validator’s rebuild: they agree until Aug 17; on Aug 18 the number before drops and the rebuild stays level. “A chart that recovers is not yet a repair: the validator’s checks below decide.”
